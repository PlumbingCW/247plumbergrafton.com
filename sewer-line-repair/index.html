Sewer backup: what to do while you wait

A sewer backup is different from a clogged sink. Wastewater is returning from the main line toward the lowest opening in the house. Stop all water use, close the lid on toilets, and move anything you can from the floor. Do not wade through the water in bare feet, and wear gloves and boots if you must go in.

  • Do not flush, run the shower, wash clothes or run the dishwasher.
  • Avoid chemical cleaners, which do not fix structural breaks and add hazard.
  • Photograph the backup and the floor for insurance.
  • Call us and say whether the backup reached floor drains, a tub or a toilet.

How a sewer line fails

Sewer lines fail slowly and then suddenly. Clay tile and old cast iron crack and shift. Joints open and let roots in. Plastic lines can sag into a belly that holds water and waste. A line that serves a house for decades can still have one bad joint that causes repeat backups.

A camera inspection tells the story. The camera shows roots, cracks, offsets, scale and any standing water. We record it and show you what it found, with a distance from the cleanout so the problem can be located above ground. Our sewer inspection service uses the same equipment as a standalone visit.

Spot repair, lining or replacement

Not every bad sewer line needs full replacement. A single break or open joint can be repaired as a section. A longer stretch with cracks or small leaks may suit relining, if the host pipe is sound enough. A collapsed or heavily offset line usually has to be dug and replaced. We explain which category your camera footage fits and why.

Digging in New England means coordinating utilities and frost conditions, and in many cases a permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Grafton building department about your address. A licensed plumber files what applies. Our crew also tells you how the work affects landscaping and driveways, so there are no surprises.

Sewers and septic in Grafton

Part of Grafton is on town sewer and part relies on private septic systems, so the first question on a backup is which one you have. A town sewer backup may involve the town line in the street, and a call to the town can establish whether it is the municipal main or your private lateral.

A septic system backup involves the tank, the distribution box and the leach field, and calls for different specialists. See drain cleaning for clogs that stay inside the house.

The Massachusetts Title 5 rules govern septic systems, and the Grafton Board of Health is the local contact for septic questions. We tell you plainly if your problem sits outside plumbing and needs a septic contractor.

Questions about sewer line repair in Grafton

Do you handle sewer emergencies at night or on weekends in Grafton?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Stop water use and call.

How do I know if the problem is the town sewer or my line?

If only your house is affected, it is usually your lateral. If neighbors are also backing up, call the town. A camera can show where the blockage is.

Can a sewer line be repaired without digging up the yard?

Sometimes. Liners can fix cracks without full excavation if the pipe is suitable. A camera tells us.

Is a sewer smell always a sewer line problem?

No. Dry traps and vent issues also cause odors. We check those first.

What is the difference between sewer repair and drain cleaning?

Cleaning removes a clog. Repair fixes a break or defect. If cleaning does not hold, repair may be needed.
