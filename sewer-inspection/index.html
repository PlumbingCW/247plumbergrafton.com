What a camera inspection shows

A flexible cable with a camera head enters the line through a cleanout. As it moves, it records the pipe wall, joints, bends and anything in the way. Roots look like hairy masses. Cracks show as dark lines. Offsets show as a step at a joint. A belly shows as standing water.

We note the distance from the cleanout for each finding, so the plumber, a contractor or a buyer can find the spot above ground. We explain what we see in plain language and tell you what is cosmetic and what is not.

If you live in one of the mill villages, Fisherville or Farnumsville, your house may be among the many in town built across a century or more. A camera shows what is actually under the street side of the house.

When an inspection is worth booking

  • Before buying a house, especially an older one.
  • After two or more clogs in a year.
  • When a plumber cannot get a cable past a point.
  • After roots were cut and you want to know how bad it is.
  • Before relining or replacing, to plan the work.

Buyers in Grafton benefit particularly in older areas such as homes near the historic districts of Fisherville and Farnumsville. After the inspection

You get the footage and a plain summary. If the line is sound, you have a baseline. If there is a defect, we explain the options and the order of urgency. Many problems can wait. Some cannot, and we say which. When repair is needed, sewer line repair is the next step, and the same crew handles it.

Some Grafton homes are on septic rather than town sewer. We can camera the building line to the tank, but the tank and field need a septic professional. We tell you where our work ends.

Inspection before you buy a house

A home inspection rarely includes the sewer line, yet it can hide the largest repair bill in the house. A camera inspection before closing shows roots, sags, cracks and offset joints, and the recording gives you something concrete to bring to a negotiation. If the home is old, has mature trees near the line or has a history of slow drains, the camera visit is a small cost against a large risk.

Locating the line

Many inspections also locate the pipe from above ground with a transmitter on the camera head. That tells you where the line runs under your lawn or driveway, which helps with planting, fences and any future dig. Call 811 before any digging so public utilities are marked as well.
