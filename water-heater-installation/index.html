Choosing the right water heater for your Grafton home

Size is the first decision. A household of two with one bathroom needs a different unit from a family of six with a dishwasher, washing machine and teenagers. We look at your peak demand, not just the number of bedrooms. A tank that is too small runs out. A tank that is too large costs more to heat.

What an installation involves

  • Shut off water and power or gas, drain the old tank, and disconnect it.
  • Set the new unit, connect water lines, install new shutoff valves where needed.
  • Connect gas or electric, and vent the unit properly.
  • Install or replace the expansion tank and the relief valve and discharge line.
  • Fill, purge air, light or power the unit, and test for leaks and temperature.

Every step is done by a licensed plumber under the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. You can verify any plumber through the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters license lookup. Permit rules vary, so ask the Grafton building department. The town has online permitting. If your old unit failed because of age, water heater replacement covers the emergency path.

Water quality, sediment and your new unit

A new tank lasts longer when the water entering it is managed. Grafton Water District and South Grafton Water District supply groundwater from wells, and homes outside those districts use private wells. Water quality differs, and we do not quote a hardness figure because we have not measured yours. A simple test kit or your water district annual report tells you what you have.

If your water is hard, a softener or a descaling plan protects the new unit. If your water has sediment, a flush each year is cheap insurance. We tell you what to expect and put it on a card by the heater.

Installing in older and newer Grafton homes

Older homes, including those near the Fisherville and Farnumsville historic districts, can have chimney venting that no longer fits a modern unit, or galvanized fittings that fail when disturbed. Newer homes along Route 140 and Route 122 usually have easier installs but may have tight utility closets. We look before we quote.

If you are also upgrading a bathroom or adding a laundry, ask about plumbing installation so the work is planned together.

Expansion tanks, pans and code details

Closed plumbing systems need somewhere for expanding hot water to go, which is the job of the expansion tank. A drain pan with a line to a safe drain limits damage if the tank fails above living space. Relief valve discharge pipes must be sized and routed correctly. These small items are easy to skip on a quick swap, and they matter on the day something goes wrong.

Questions about water heater installation in Grafton

How long does a water heater installation take?

A like-for-like swap is often a few hours. A change of fuel, venting or unit type takes longer.

Do you remove the old water heater?

Yes, as part of the job.

Do I need a permit?

Permit rules vary. Ask the Grafton building department. A licensed plumber will advise.

Can you move my water heater?

Often yes. Moving a unit changes lines and venting, so we quote it separately.

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night?

Yes, the 24/7 line is open. See our emergency replacement page for a failed tank.
