Burst pipe: the first ten minutes

Speed matters more than anything else. Find the main valve, usually where the water line enters the house, and turn it fully off. Then open the faucet at the lowest point to drain what is left in the pipes. If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, stay out of the wet area and switch off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything.
  • Move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water path.
  • Do not use a heat gun or open flame on a frozen pipe.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell us where the water entered and whether it has stopped.

If you do not know where the shutoff is, say so on the phone. We can describe where to look, and a plumber can close the street valve only through the water utility, so inside valves matter.

Why pipes burst in Grafton winters

New England freeze cycles are the main driver. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, crawlspaces, porches and under bay windows are at risk. Frozen water expands, and the split often shows up when the ice thaws and water flows again. A sudden drip or hiss after a cold night usually means the pipe split during the freeze.

Other causes: corrosion at joints in older copper and galvanized lines, high water pressure that stresses fittings, and an impact from a nail or a drill. Houses with a vacation season or a long unheated stretch carry more risk. If a home will be empty in winter, a plumber can drain the system.

Repair, then prevention

Repair starts with cutting out the damaged section and installing a new length with proper fittings. We check for further damage nearby, since a pipe that froze once often froze in a second spot too. Then we pressure-test the line before it is closed up.

Prevention is practical. Insulate pipes in cold spaces, seal drafts near water lines, and keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze. A slow drip is a stopgap, not a plan. Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots before winter. If you want help finding weak spots, our leak detection visits include checking the lines most likely to fail.

After repair, drying the area quickly matters as much as the pipe itself. We explain what to ask a drying contractor and what to photograph for your insurance claim.

Burst pipes in older and newer Grafton homes

Older houses in Grafton, including those in and around the Fisherville and Farnumsville historic districts, often have mixed piping from decades of repairs. Newer homes along the Route 140 and Route 122 corridors typically use PEX, which handles freezing better than rigid copper but is not immune. Homes on private wells should also check the well pressure tank and the line from the well house.

Landlords and property managers can call for tenant emergencies. We explain what happened and what the fix covered, which helps with insurers and tenants. A rental left cold over a holiday weekend is a classic burst scenario.

Questions about burst pipe repair in Grafton

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Grafton?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Shut off the main water valve first and then call us.

Where is my main water shutoff?

It is usually where the water line enters the house, often in a basement or utility area. If you cannot find it, tell us and we will walk you through it.

Will insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies cover sudden water damage but not always the pipe. Photograph everything and call your insurer early.

Can a frozen pipe be thawed safely?

Slow warmth such as a hair dryer on low can work if the pipe has not burst. Never use an open flame. If water is already leaking, shut off the main and call.

Should I leave taps dripping in a freeze?

A slow drip keeps water moving in at-risk lines, but it is not a guarantee. Insulation and closing drafts do more.
