Is tankless right for your home?

Tankless is a good match when you want continuous hot water, have limited floor space, or are replacing an aging tank and can upgrade gas and venting. It is a weaker match if your gas line is undersized, your home has no suitable vent path, or you want the lowest upfront price. We lay out both cases.

  • Gas line size: tankless units pull a lot of gas at once.
  • Venting: usually a different vent type than a tank.
  • Flow rate: how many fixtures run together.
  • Water quality: scale is the main enemy of the heat exchanger.
  • Electric tankless: panel capacity and circuit sizing.

Our water heater installation page covers the tank side of the decision.

Flush and descale: the service that saves the unit

Scale builds up on the heat exchanger and narrows the passages. Symptoms include fluctuating temperature, error codes, reduced flow and noises. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit to dissolve buildup, then rinses it clean. We also clean the inlet screen, check the venting and test combustion on gas units.

How often depends on your water. Grafton Water District and South Grafton Water District supply groundwater from wells, and private wells vary by site. We do not quote a hardness number for the town, so test your tap or ask your water district for its annual report, and set a schedule from that.

Installing tankless in Grafton

An install can involve a new gas line, a new vent, a drain for condensate, and electrical power for the controls. We check each before we quote. Older homes near the historic districts of Fisherville and Farnumsville sometimes have tight spaces or old chimneys that do not suit modern venting. Newer homes along Route 140 and Route 122 are usually easier.

Tank to tankless: what changes in the house

Swapping a tank for tankless often changes more than the heater. The gas line may need a larger size, the vent usually changes, and a condensate drain may be needed. Electric tankless units may need a large new circuit. You also gain wall space, since tankless units hang on the wall. Ask for the full scope in writing so the quote includes the gas, vent and drain, not only the unit.

Questions before you commit

Ask about gas line size, vent type, condensate drain, flushing and warranty before you buy. A good installer answers all five.

Maintenance schedule at a glance

Plan an annual flush, a check of the inlet screen, an inspection of the vent and a look at the condensate drain on condensing units. Mark the dates on the unit or in a calendar. A few quiet visits are the cheapest way to avoid an emergency one.

Questions about tankless water heater service in Grafton

How often should a tankless water heater be flushed?

It depends on your water. Many manufacturers suggest annual flushing. We can set a schedule once you know your water quality.

Can you repair any brand of tankless water heater?

We diagnose most brands. Parts availability varies by model.

Why does my tankless unit give cold bursts?

Often scale, a flow sensor, or low flow. A service visit finds it.

Do you handle tankless emergencies at night or on weekends in Grafton?

Yes, the 24/7 line is open. We troubleshoot by phone first.

Will a tankless unit work on a private well?

It can, with correct filtration and sizing. We discuss it on the visit.
