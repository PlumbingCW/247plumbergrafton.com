The village and its streets

Farnumsville is a 19th-century mill village on the east bank of the Blackstone River in Grafton. The National Register district runs along Providence Street and Main Street, roughly between Cross Street and Depot Street, and spills onto the streets beside them. The village is centered on the Farnum Mill, which began operating in the 1810s. The surviving main mill building dates to 1844.

The Farnumsville Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 16, 1996.

Housing from the 1820s to the 1920s

The National Register record puts village housing between about the 1820s and the 1920s, in a diversity of styles. The state survey names worker cottages and tenements, double-chimney rows, Italianate duplexes and four-family buildings on Harding Street, along with a boardinghouse and a church from 1862.

Because the span is so wide, two houses on the same block can differ by decades. That affects how a plumber should plan a job. A kitchen added in the 1960s and a bathroom added in the 1990s can share a wall with fixtures from much earlier, and different eras often mean different piping. The honest approach is to look at what is actually there.

Remodeling in a house with several layers of history

Many Farnumsville homes have been altered over the years, and a bathroom or kitchen remodel is where past changes show up. Opening a wall can reveal pipes of several materials joined at different dates. Before you commit to a layout, ask the plumber to look at the supply lines and drains that the new fixtures will connect to, and to explain what can be reused and what should be replaced.

For a larger project, plumbing installation covers new fixture runs, and repiping is worth discussing if you find mixed materials throughout. Get the scope and price in writing, and ask who pulls the permit.

Basements, drains and the river nearby

We did not find a published record of flood or groundwater conditions for Farnumsville, so we make no claim about them. What applies to any home near a river is practical. Know where your floor drain and any sump pump discharge go, and test a pump before heavy rain rather than after.

If a basement drain backs up or a pump runs constantly, see sump pump repair and drain cleaning. If drains slow down in more than one fixture, a sewer camera inspection can show whether the problem is in the house line or farther out.

Which utility serves you

The South Grafton Water District draws from wells off Providence Road and Ferry Street, and the Grafton Water District serves other parts of town. Your bill shows which one is yours. The same state report says sewer coverage is incomplete in some residential areas near those wells, so confirm your own sewer status with the Town of Grafton.

Questions about plumbing in Farnumsville

Where exactly is Farnumsville?

It is a village in Grafton on the east bank of the Blackstone River. The historic district centers on Providence Street and Main Street between Cross and Depot Streets.

Does a house from the 1800s need all its pipes replaced?

Not automatically. Age alone does not decide it. A plumber should inspect the lines, and a camera can check the drain. Replacement makes sense when the pipes are failing or when a remodel exposes mixed materials.

Who pulls the permit for plumbing work?

In Massachusetts the plumber normally applies for the permit. Ask who will do it before work starts, and ask the Town of Grafton which permits apply at your address.

Is my Farnumsville house on town sewer?

Check with the Town of Grafton before you plan any drain work. A state water report says sewer coverage is incomplete in some residential areas near the nearby district wells.

Can I use this page to confirm my flood risk?

No. We found no published flood data for the village, so this page makes no flood claim. Check official flood maps and your insurer for that.
