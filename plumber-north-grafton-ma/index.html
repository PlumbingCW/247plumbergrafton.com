What North Grafton includes

North Grafton is the commuter-rail village on the northwest side of town. Its ZIP code is 01536. The MBTA Framingham/Worcester Line stops at Grafton station here. Worcester Street, which is Route 122, is the main western gateway into Grafton, and Exit 11 of the Massachusetts Turnpike is about a mile from the area covered by a 2017 state planning study.

The older part of North Grafton is sometimes called New England Village or Northeast Village. It grew around a textile mill that opened in 1826 on the Quinsigamond River, with worker cottages, tenement rows and boarding houses on Factory Street and Second Street. We cover that area on this page and do not treat it as a separate neighborhood.

Postwar homes on small lots

The 2017 Worcester Street strategic plan describes the neighborhoods off this stretch of Route 122 as postwar areas. Most are small single-family Cape Cod and ranch homes built in the late 1940s and early 1950s, usually on lots under a quarter acre. The plan calls them the largest cluster of postwar neighborhoods in Grafton.

Many of these houses are now 70 years old or more, and some have been updated since. The study does not describe their plumbing, so we do not either. What the era suggests is the questions worth asking: how old your water heater is, where the main shutoff sits, and whether a past owner finished part of the basement.

Condos, apartments and shared systems

The same study lists condominiums and apartments near Worcester Street, including Hovey Pond Estates, Northgate Court, Rosewood Gardens and Grafton Gardens, plus one-to-four-family houses on Worcester Street itself. Shared buildings raise a question that a single-family house does not, which is who owns the pipe.

In a condo, the association usually owns the shared stacks and mains, while the unit owner is responsible for fixtures inside the unit. The exact line depends on your governing documents, so read them or ask your manager before you call anyone. A plumber can tell you where the problem is. Your documents tell you who pays.

Water, sewer and a changing neighborhood

The Grafton Water District draws water from wells in town, including two off East Street in North Grafton and one off Worcester Street. The state source water report lists potassium hydroxide treatment for pH and sodium hypochlorite for disinfection. The planning study says its project area is served by town water and sewer. It does not say the same for the whole village, so check your own address.

The former Grafton State Hospital site at 8 Pine Street is the subject of a state-listed plan for 316 rental apartments and a small retail village. The state page labels it proposed and gives no construction status. If you are buying or renting near it, ask the owner or the town what has actually been built.

Questions about plumbing in North Grafton

Is North Grafton part of Grafton?

Yes. It is a village within the Town of Grafton with its own ZIP code, 01536. The older mill area is sometimes called New England Village or Northeast Village.

What kind of homes are in North Grafton?

A 2017 state study describes the neighborhoods off Route 122 as mostly small Cape Cod and ranch homes from the late 1940s and early 1950s, along with condos and apartments. It also notes older mill-village housing near the river.

Who fixes a pipe in my condo?

It depends on your condo documents. Associations commonly handle shared lines and owners handle fixtures inside the unit. Read the documents or ask your manager first.

Is the former state hospital site built yet?

The state lists a proposed development with 316 rental units and a retail village at 8 Pine Street. It gives no construction status, so check with the town for current progress.

Does Grafton have a plumbing inspector?

Plumbing work generally needs a permit under state rules. Ask the Grafton building department which permits apply at your address.
