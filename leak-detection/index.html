Signs of a hidden leak and what to do right now

People call us for a handful of reasons. The water meter keeps moving after every tap is closed. The water bill jumped without a change in habits. A ceiling stain spreads after the shower runs. A floor feels warm in one patch. You hear running water in a quiet house. Any of these deserves attention.

  • Check the meter: turn off every tap and appliance, and watch whether the dial or the leak indicator moves.
  • Check the toilets with a few drops of food coloring in the tank.
  • If water is visibly coming through a ceiling, shut off the main and turn off the breaker for the nearest lights.
  • Photograph damage for your insurer before cleanup.

Meter running with everything off: what it means

A moving meter with all fixtures closed means water is leaving the house plumbing somewhere. The leak could be in the house, between the meter and the foundation, or in an outdoor line to a hose bib, irrigation system or detached garage. We isolate the zone first: close the main valve inside the house, and if the meter keeps running, the leak is on the supply line between the street and your valve.

If the meter stops when the main is closed, the leak is inside. We then narrow it with acoustic listening on pipes and fixtures, and when needed, thermal imaging for hot lines and moisture checks on walls and floors. We explain what the equipment is detecting rather than just handing over a number.

Ceilings, walls, crawlspaces and under-slab leaks

Water follows gravity and framing, so the stain is rarely under the leak. A wet bathroom ceiling might come from the shower valve above, a drain line, or the roof. We test the plumbing first, then say plainly if the roof or condensation looks more likely.

Leaks in Grafton homes

Housing in Grafton ranges from older homes near the historic districts of Fisherville and Farnumsville to newer builds across the town. Older copper and galvanized lines fail at joints and elbows, while newer PEX fails less often but can be damaged by nails or frozen water. In a Massachusetts winter, a burst or split from freezing is the most common sudden leak, and we cover that on our burst pipe repair page.

Homes on town water have a meter, which makes diagnosis easier. Homes on private wells have no meter, so we use pressure drop tests and listen at the well pressure tank. If your well pump cycles on when nobody is using water, tell us on the phone.

Landlords and property managers in Grafton can call the same line. A written report describing the source and the repair helps with tenants and insurers, and we describe our findings in plain language.

Questions about leak detection in Grafton

Do you handle leak detection emergencies at night or on weekends in Grafton?

Yes. If water is actively entering your home, call any hour. Shut off the main water valve while you wait.

Will you cut holes in my walls to find the leak?

We aim to pinpoint first and open only the necessary spot. If an access panel is the only way to confirm, we tell you before cutting.

My water bill went up but I see no leak. Where do I start?

Check the meter with everything off. Check toilets for silent leaks. If the meter still moves, call us.

Can you find a leak under a concrete slab?

Often yes, with listening gear and thermal imaging for hot water lines. We explain the likely repair options after we pinpoint it.

Can you find a leak on a private well system?

Yes. We test pressure behavior at the tank and use listening equipment on the lines. No meter is required.
