Main line blocked: how it shows up

Signs of a blocked main include more than one fixture draining slowly, a toilet that gurgles when you run the shower, and water coming up in the lowest tub or floor drain. Another sign is a repeating pattern: the line clears after a snake and clogs again in a month.

If sewage is coming up, treat it as an emergency. Stop using water, keep people away, and call. Once water use stops, the backup usually stops rising, which buys time for the plumber to arrive.

What we use to clean a main line

We start with a cable machine through a cleanout. Cutting heads break through roots and compacted waste. For grease, scale or repeated clogs, we add hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall with high pressure water. Before jetting, a camera confirms the pipe is sound enough to take the pressure.

  • Cable machine with cutting heads for roots and compacted waste.
  • Hydro jetter for grease, scale and heavy buildup.
  • Camera before and after, with footage you can keep.
  • Cleanout installation if your line does not have one.

Many homes lack a cleanout, which makes every service call harder. Adding one is a small job with a big payoff. See sewer inspection for what the camera lets us confirm.

Keeping the line open

Roots return to joints that keep leaking, so a one-time cleaning is a repair for the symptom, not the cause. Some homeowners put a root-cutting visit on a schedule. Others repair the joint and stop the cycle. Our crew explains both honestly, including when scheduled cleaning is the cheaper path.

Kitchen grease sent down the drain is the other common cause in the main. Wipe pans before washing, and keep wipes, even those labeled flushable, out of toilets. Septic owners should also avoid additives and harsh chemicals that can upset the system.

After the line is cleared

Ask the plumber what came out of the pipe. Roots suggest a cracked joint. Grease suggests kitchen habits. Wipes suggest a household rule. Knowing the cause tells you whether this was a one-time event or a pattern, and whether a camera look is wise.

Questions about sewer line cleaning in Grafton

Do you handle sewer line cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Grafton?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Stop using water and call.

How often should a main sewer line be cleaned?

Only when it needs it, unless roots keep returning. Repeat clogs suggest scheduling or repair.

Will cleaning the line damage it?

Done correctly, no. We camera first so we do not jet a failing pipe.

Can I clean the main myself with a rented snake?

You can try, but a rented cable can get stuck or damage a pipe. A plumber also sees the cause.

Why does my sewer line clog every year?

Usually roots or a belly in the pipe. A camera shows which.
