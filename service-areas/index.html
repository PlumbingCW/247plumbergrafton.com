Inside Grafton

Our home base is Grafton, ZIP 01519 and the surrounding Grafton ZIP codes. The dispatch line covers every part of town, including North Grafton, South Grafton, Fisherville and Farnumsville. Older mill-era homes in the historic districts and newer houses along Route 140 and Route 122 are both familiar ground.

Nearby places we also reach

Hopkinton is a short drive east of Westborough and is covered for emergency calls. Worcester borders Millbury and Shrewsbury, and Whitinsville is a village of Northbridge. If you are not sure whether your address is covered, call and the dispatcher will tell you right away.

Westborough

Westborough is a commuter town along Route 9 and I-90. Houses are away for long hours, so slow leaks go unnoticed. Check your main shutoff and read the Westborough emergency plumber page for local details.

Shrewsbury

Shrewsbury is large and varied, with condos, lakeside homes and busy commercial strips. Leaks travel between units and drains serve many fixtures. Our Shrewsbury emergency plumber page covers what to do first.

Millbury

Millbury lies close to Grafton along the Blackstone River. Older homes and low ground near the river shape its calls. See the Millbury emergency plumber page.

Northbridge

Northbridge has several villages, including Whitinsville and Rockdale, with mill-era housing and small businesses. The Northbridge emergency plumber page lists both ZIP codes.

Upton

Upton is rural, with many wells and septic systems. The Upton emergency plumber page explains how well and septic emergencies differ.

How dispatch works across towns

One number reaches the dispatcher for every town. Call (508) 389-4045, give your address and describe the problem. The dispatcher tells you what to shut off and gives you an honest window for arrival. Response depends on traffic, weather and call volume, so we do not promise a fixed time.

What changes from town to town

Water and sewer arrangements differ across the area. Some homes use town water and sewer, and others rely on private wells and septic systems. Grafton is served by the Grafton Water District and the South Grafton Water District, and homes outside those districts use wells. Upton is more rural, so wells and septic come up more often there. Shrewsbury and Westborough have more town services and denser housing. Millbury and Northbridge have mill-era homes with older pipe.

Knowing which system you have helps us bring the right parts. Tell the dispatcher if you are on a well, on septic or on town water and sewer.

If your town is not listed

Plumbing emergencies do not stop at a town line. If you live near Grafton but outside the towns above, call the 24/7 line anyway. Tell the dispatcher your address and the problem. We will tell you at once whether we can reach you and, if not, we will say so plainly so you can call someone else without losing time.

Common calls by season

Winter brings frozen pipes, boilers that quit and sump pumps that freeze. Spring brings wet basements and failing pumps during thaw and storms. Summer brings water heater and drain calls as households use more water, and fall is the best time for planned work such as heater swaps and repiping. Knowing the season helps you prepare, and our service pages explain each problem.

Choosing a plumber you can trust

  • Check the license on the state lookup before work starts.
  • Ask for the price and the scope in plain words before you agree.
  • Ask what caused the problem and how to stop it recurring.
  • Ask who pulls permits when they are required.
  • Keep the invoice, which helps with insurance and resale.

We are glad to answer all of these before we start, and we think every homeowner should ask them of any plumber.

Permits and licensing by town

Plumbing is licensed work under the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. Permit rules are set by each town, so ask the local building department about your address. You can check any plumber through the state license lookup. We encourage you to do that before you hire anyone, including us.
