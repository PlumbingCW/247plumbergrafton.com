Planning new plumbing before you build

Plumbing is the part of a remodel that is hardest to change later. Before walls close, we review where water supplies, drains and vents need to go, how far fixtures sit from the main stack, and what your existing system can carry. A good plan avoids surprises and keeps the project on schedule.

ProjectWhat we check
New bathroom.Drain slope, vent path, supply capacity, floor framing.
Kitchen remodel.Sink location, dishwasher drain, disposal, gas or water to appliances.
Laundry room.Standpipe, supply valves, drain size, hot water access.
Basement bath.Sewage ejector or below-grade drain, vent routing.
Outdoor spigot.Frost-free sillcock and shutoff inside.

Homeowners often buy fixtures before calling a plumber. That is fine, but share model numbers early. Some fixtures need specific rough-in valves or clearances that must be set before the wall is closed. We check and tell you what is needed.

Code, permits and inspection

New plumbing work is governed by the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. A licensed plumber pulls permits where required, and inspections protect you at resale. Ask the Grafton building department about requirements for your address. You can verify any plumber through the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters license lookup.

If your new fixture shares a line with an old one, we also check that the drain can handle it. Slow drains in an old house often show up after a remodel. See drain cleaning if that is your case.

Installing in Grafton homes new and old

Houses along Route 140 and Route 122 range from recent builds to older farmhouses, and the historic districts of Fisherville and Farnumsville have homes with original piping behind the walls. Older plumbing can influence your new work, since we may need to tie in to galvanized, lead-free but aging copper, or cast iron. We look before we quote.

Homes on septic should confirm tank capacity before adding a bathroom. Grafton Board of Health sets local septic rules, so ask them first. If your whole system is failing, look at whole-house repiping.

Appliance hookups that need the right valve

Washing machines, dishwashers, ice makers and utility sinks each need a supply valve and a drain that fits. A washer box with a standpipe at the correct height prevents siphoning. A dishwasher needs a high loop or an air gap. An ice maker line needs a proper tap and a shutoff you can reach. These are small details that decide whether an appliance runs for years or floods a cabinet.

Bring the plumber in early

The best time to call is before the cabinets are ordered. Fixture size, rough-in height and drain location affect which products fit. A shower valve set at the wrong depth cannot accept a tile that is too thick. Share the plans, the fixture model numbers and the tile thickness, and we will tell you what to change before it becomes expensive.

If the project reveals old pipe that should be replaced while the wall is open, we say so and price it separately so you can decide. Opening a wall twice is the most expensive way to fix plumbing.
