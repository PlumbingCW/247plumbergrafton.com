Sump pump not working: what to do first

If water is rising, safety comes first. Do not enter standing water near outlets, power strips or the electrical panel. If the basement floor is wet and electrical items are submerged, stay out and call an electrician or the utility. When the area is safe, check that the pump is plugged in, the breaker has not tripped, and the float moves freely.

  • Unplug the pump before touching it.
  • Lift the float by hand. If the pump runs, the float is the problem.
  • Check that the discharge line is not frozen or kinked.
  • Check the check valve for a stuck flap.
  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit to test.

Most pumps last years, not decades. A pump with age, rust or noise is a candidate for replacement before the next storm. If the problem is bigger than the pump, see basement waterproofing.

Battery backup and alarms

Storms often cause power outages, which is when sump pumps matter most. A battery backup keeps the system running when the grid fails. A high-water alarm warns you before the basement floods. Both are inexpensive compared to a flooded finished basement.

A water-powered backup is another option in some homes, and we tell you the trade-offs. Battery systems need replacement batteries over time, so test them each season.

Sump pumps in Grafton

Grafton sits in the Blackstone River watershed, and sump pumps in low areas near Fisherville and Farnumsville can run heavily during spring melt and storms. Discharge lines must be routed away from the foundation and out of the way of neighbors and wetlands. Check Grafton regulations before connecting any discharge to a sewer or septic. Connecting a sump pump to a sanitary sewer is generally not allowed, so ask the town before you change it.

Winter can freeze a discharge line. We use grades and outlets that drain, and we show you how to check yours.

Choosing a replacement pump

Submersible pumps sit in the pit and run quietly. Pedestal pumps sit above it and are easier to service but louder. Horsepower should match the water you move, not the largest number on the box. A pump that is too big short cycles, and a pump that is too small runs without catching up in a storm. We size to your pit, your lift height and the discharge run.

Testing before storm season

Pour a bucket of water into the pit each spring and fall and watch the pump start, empty the pit and stop. If it hesitates, call before the rain does.
