Faucet or valve will not shut off

If water is running and the handle does not stop it, go to the shutoff valves under the sink. Turn them clockwise. If they are stuck, the main valve is the fallback. A stuck shutoff valve is itself a repair to schedule, since the next emergency will be worse.

  • Bucket under any leaking connection.
  • Towels around the base of the cabinet.
  • Photograph damage before cleanup.
  • Do not force a corroded valve, since it can snap and create a bigger leak.

Repair or replace

Cartridges and seals are often cheap and fast to replace. A faucet with corroded body, bad finish or discontinued parts is better replaced. We carry common sizes and bring parts for typical brands, and we tell you if your fixture is not worth another repair.

Replacing a faucet also lets us check the supply lines and shutoffs. Braided lines and quarter-turn valves are a small upgrade that prevents most under-sink emergencies. If a drain or trap is involved, it may tie into drain cleaning.

Shower and tub valves

A shower that will not turn off, a handle that spins without effect or a tub spout that sends water to the shower head when you want the tub all point to the valve inside the wall. Some valves have a replaceable cartridge reachable from the front.

Others need an access panel on the back side of the wall. We tell you which you have before we cut anything, and we leave a clean access panel so the next repair is easy.

Choosing a faucet you will not regret

Ceramic disc cartridges outlast older compression designs, and a solid brass body outlasts a plastic one. Pick a model with parts that are easy to find in the store and not only by special order. A cheap faucet that drips in a year will cost more than a good one that does not.
