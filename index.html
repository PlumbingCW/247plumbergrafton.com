Plumbing in Grafton

What makes plumbing in Grafton different

Grafton mixes old and new. Mill-era housing around the historic districts of Fisherville and Farnumsville sits close to newer neighborhoods along Route 140 and Route 122. Older homes can hide galvanized supply pipe and cast iron drains, which wear from the inside. If your pipes have leaked more than once, ask about repiping before the next winter.

Water comes from different places too. The Grafton Water District and the South Grafton Water District supply parts of town from groundwater, and other homes use private wells. Each district can give you its annual water quality report, which tells you far more about your water than a guess. That matters for water heaters, which fail sooner when minerals build up. Our emergency water heater replacement page explains the fast route.

Grafton sits in the Blackstone River watershed, so low ground near the brooks can see wet basements after storms. A working sump pump is the first defense. If yours fails during a storm, our sump pump repair page tells you what to do.

Winters here are cold enough to freeze an exposed pipe in hours. If the heat goes out, read our boiler repair page, and if a pipe has split, burst pipe repair explains the first minutes. All plumbing work follows the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, and you can verify any plumber through the state license lookup.

North Grafton, home to the Tufts Cummings School of Veterinary Medicine, and South Grafton add more variety, from rental housing to small businesses that need after-hours commercial plumbing.
