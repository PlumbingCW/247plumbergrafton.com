Disposal jammed or leaking: safe first steps

Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off. Switch it off at the wall switch and, if possible, at the breaker. A humming disposal that does not turn is jammed. A disposal that runs but leaks is failing at a seal. A disposal that is quiet may have tripped its reset button.

  • Turn off power before any inspection.
  • Use tongs or pliers for objects, never fingers.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom if it hummed and stopped.
  • Bucket under any leak, then call us.

Replacing a disposal

Replacement is the better choice for a cracked housing or an old motor. We match horsepower to your use, connect the dishwasher drain, and test. If the sink has two basins, we confirm the drain layout and the air gap or high loop for the dishwasher hose.

Homes on septic should use food waste sparingly, since solids load the tank. Ask us if a disposal suits your system. A kitchen remodel is a good time to coordinate with plumbing installation.

Clogs behind the disposal

A disposal that grinds fine but leaves the sink full is not a disposal problem. Food waste ends up in the trap and the branch line, where grease turns it into a plug. Running a disposal harder will not clear it.

We pull the trap, clear the line behind it and check that the trap arm slopes correctly, since a flat or sagging trap holds waste. If the problem comes back every few weeks, we look at the line further down.

Before you replace a unit, see whether the sink drain is the cause. Drain cleaning sometimes saves the cost of a new disposal.

Quick prevention

Run cold water while grinding, avoid fibrous scraps and never pour grease into the sink. Those habits extend the life of any disposal.
