The center around the common

Grafton Center sits on a hill west of the Quinsigamond River, around an oval town common laid out in 1728. National Register listing for the Grafton Common Historic District dates from June 22, 1988. Most buildings in it date to the middle of the 19th century, and the enclosing fence dates to 1845. The district includes the Grafton Inn, built in 1805, and two churches.

The center is a mix of homes, civic and religious buildings and a few businesses. Residential streets run out from the common along North, South, Worcester, Oak and Millbury Streets, according to the state survey. A state community profile notes that houses on North and South Streets were mostly built between 1800 and 1850.

Houses built before modern plumbing

The survey lists center-chimney houses, Federal and Greek Revival buildings, a Second Empire block, Queen Anne houses, Four Squares and Colonial Revival homes. That is a wide range. Houses that were first built before indoor plumbing existed have been fitted with bathrooms and kitchens at later dates, so the layout of pipes may follow the history of the building more than a plan.

That is a reason to look before you decide. A plumber who has seen the actual lines can tell you what to keep and what to replace. A sewer camera inspection shows the condition of the drain to the street, and leak detection can trace a hidden problem without opening walls first.

Renovating in an older house

Plan the plumbing early in a renovation. Think about where a new bathroom or kitchen can connect to existing supply and drain lines, how a hot water source will reach it, and whether the home heats with a boiler. Planning early keeps the plumber from cutting into finished work later.

ProjectQuestions to settle first
A new or moved bathroom.Where the drain and vent will run, and what the floor framing allows.
A new water heater.Where it will sit, how it vents and whether the house gas or electric service can support it.
A kitchen remodel.Whether supply and drain lines can be reused, and where the shutoffs are.
A boiler or heating change.Whether the existing piping suits the new equipment.

See plumbing installation, water heater installation and boiler repair for the work most often involved.

Before you call a plumber

  • Note the age of the house and any bathroom or kitchen additions you know about.
  • Find the main shutoff and the water heater so you can point to them.
  • Photograph the problem and write down when it started.
  • Do not cut into or move old pipes yourself, and avoid using a fixture that is leaking.
  • Ask for the scope of work and the price in writing before you approve a repair.

Historic character and local rules

National Register listing recognizes a place and does not by itself decide what an owner may do. Local rules are a separate matter. If your home is in the center, ask the Town of Grafton whether any review applies before you plan visible exterior work, and ask which permits apply to the plumbing. Do not assume either answer.

Grafton has two public water systems and some private wells. Check your water bill to see which one serves you, and confirm sewer or septic with the town before you plan any drain work.

Questions about plumbing in Grafton Center

Where is Grafton Center?

It is the historic center of the town, around Grafton Common, on a hill west of the Quinsigamond River. Its ZIP code is 01519.

Is Grafton Center the same as the National Register district?

The Grafton Common Historic District covers the common and surrounding historic buildings. The town center extends along the radial streets, and we do not claim an exact boundary.

Do old houses always need new plumbing?

No. Age alone does not decide it. A plumber should inspect the lines, and a camera can check the drain. Replace what is failing or in the way of your project.

Does National Register listing limit my renovation?

Listing alone does not set rules for a private owner. Ask the Town of Grafton whether any local review applies to your property.

Where can I verify a plumber's license?

Mass.gov offers an occupational license check, linked in the sources box below. Also ask the town which permits your project needs.
