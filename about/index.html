Who we are

Grafton 24/7 Plumbing is a plumbing dispatch and service brand for Grafton, Massachusetts and the towns around it. We answer the phone around the clock, send a licensed plumber, and explain the problem and the price before any work begins.

We keep the claims modest. We do not publish review counts, years in business or response-time promises, because a promise we cannot keep is worse than none. What we can promise is how we work.

How we work

  • We answer emergencies at any hour and tell you what to shut off first.
  • We find the cause before recommending a repair.
  • We give a clear price before work starts and wait for your approval.
  • We tell you when a job needs a specialist such as a septic, structural or heating contractor.

Licensing and standards

Plumbing in Massachusetts is licensed work governed by the state plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00. You can check any plumber through the state Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters license lookup, and we encourage you to check anyone you hire. Permit rules for your address are set by the Grafton building department, which handles permits online.

What happens when you call

A dispatcher answers and asks four things. Where are you, what is happening, is the main closed, and is anyone in danger. If you smell gas, we send you outside first. Otherwise we tell you what to shut off and send a plumber.

The plumber looks at the problem, finds the cause and explains the options. You hear the price before work starts. Nothing begins until you say yes.

What we will not do

We will not promise an arrival time we cannot control. Traffic, weather and other emergencies change the schedule, so the dispatcher gives you an honest window. We will not invent reviews, awards or statistics. We will not push a replacement when a repair is sound, and we will not hide a problem that needs a different kind of contractor.

Jobs we hand to specialists

Some work belongs to other trades. Septic tank design and pumping, structural foundation repair, oil burner service, steam systems and electrical wiring all call for specialists. We tell you plainly when a job falls outside plumbing, and we explain what to ask the right contractor.

How we write our guides

The guides on this site are written for Grafton homes. Where a local fact matters, such as water supply, septic oversight or permits, we point you to the source. We name the Grafton Water District, the South Grafton Water District, the Grafton Board of Health and the Grafton building department because those are the offices that can answer address-specific questions. Read the plumbing guides for examples.

A note on records

After each visit, keep the invoice and any photographs. They show the cause, the date and the work done, and they help with insurance, warranties and resale.

Honest expectations

A plumber can usually stop an emergency on the first visit. Some repairs need a second visit for parts, drying or inspection. We tell you which category your job falls in before the first visit ends, so you can plan.

Questions we hear most

People ask whether we charge for coming out. The plumber explains any trip charge before you agree to the visit. People ask if we repair or replace. We do whichever makes sense and price both when it is close. People ask whether we can tell them the cause over the phone. Often we can guess, but we confirm it on site before recommending a fix.

People also ask whether to shut off the water at the main or at the fixture. If a fixture has its own valve and it works, use it. If water is out of control, close the main. Either way, call us and we will talk you through it.

Preparing your home before trouble starts

An hour of preparation prevents most of the damage we see. Find your main shutoff and make sure it turns. Learn where the water heater shutoff and the gas meter are. Locate your sump pump and test it. Check the supply lines under sinks and behind toilets for corrosion. Keep a flashlight and our number where you can find them in the dark.

If you rent, tell your landlord where you found the shutoff and ask for a written plan for emergencies. If you own a rental, give each tenant the same card.
