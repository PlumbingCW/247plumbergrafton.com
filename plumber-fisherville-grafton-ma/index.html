Where Fisherville sits in Grafton

Fisherville lies in the southwest part of Grafton, where the Quinsigamond River joins the Blackstone. It is a village inside the Town of Grafton and not a separate municipality. The Massachusetts Historical Commission survey describes worker housing southwest of the mill and east of Ferry Street, with houses stretching west along Main Street.

The village grew around a four-story brick mill that dates to 1831 and 1832. The Fisherville Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 16, 1996. Exact street-by-street borders are not published in the sources we found, so this page uses Ferry Street and Main Street as anchors and does not claim a precise boundary.

A village of closely built mill housing

Most of Fisherville's housing growth came in the last two decades of the 19th century. The National Register record describes a densely packed residential area of worker houses in Italianate and Queen Anne styles. The state survey adds double-chimney worker rows, duplexes and boarding houses, with duplex construction continuing into the early 20th century.

None of that tells you what is inside your walls today. Houses of the same age have been renovated many times, and pipe materials differ from one building to the next. What the building type does tell you is who shares what, and that is worth sorting out before any plumber starts work.

Common plumbing needs in an older village

Households in mixed-age buildings call about the same problems as anywhere else in Grafton. Slow or repeated drain backups point to drain cleaning or, when more than one fixture is affected, to the main line. A water heater that has reached the end of its life is a job for water heater replacement. Water stains or a bill that climbs for no clear reason call for leak detection.

If you do not know what kind of drain pipe runs from the house to the street, a sewer camera inspection shows it. The camera answers the question directly, so nobody has to guess from the age of the house.

Water and sewer: check what serves your address

Grafton has two public water systems, the Grafton Water District and the South Grafton Water District, and some homes use private wells. The state source water report for the South Grafton district lists wells off Ferry Street and off Providence Road, which is Route 122. Ferry Street runs through Fisherville, but that does not tell you which system serves a given house. Your water bill does.

The same report says that most, but not all, of the residential land in its protection areas has public sewers. Do not assume your house is on town sewer. Confirm it with the Town of Grafton before you plan any sewer work, because septic systems follow different rules than sewer connections.

Before you call a plumber

  • Find the main water shutoff and turn it clockwise if water is escaping.
  • Photograph any damage before you clean it up.
  • Write down which fixtures are affected and when the problem started.
  • Clear a path to the water heater, meter and any shutoff valves.
  • Ask for the scope of work and the price in writing before you approve a repair.

Questions about plumbing in Fisherville

Is Fisherville a separate town?

No. Fisherville is a historic mill village within the Town of Grafton, Massachusetts. Its historic district was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1996.

Which water district serves a Fisherville house?

The sources we found do not map individual streets to a district. Check your water bill or ask the South Grafton or Grafton water district directly.

Should I assume my house is on town sewer?

No. A state report notes that most but not all residential areas in the South Grafton district's protection zones have public sewers. Confirm with the Town of Grafton.

I own a duplex. What should I know before a plumber starts?

Find out which unit each shutoff, water heater and drain line serves, and tell both households if water will be off. Ask for a written scope before work begins.

How do I check a plumber's credentials?

Use the Massachusetts state license lookup linked in the sources box below, and ask the town which permits apply to your work.
