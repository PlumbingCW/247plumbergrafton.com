Clogs that come back: when jetting makes sense

A common story: someone snaked the line, it ran fine for a few weeks, and then it clogged again. A cable cuts a channel through the center of a blockage, but grease and scale cling to the pipe wall and keep narrowing the opening. Jetting cleans the full diameter.

Restaurants, multi-unit buildings and kitchens that handle a lot of grease are the classic candidates. So are older homes with scale in cast iron lines. A main line with heavy root growth may also need jetting after cutting.

How hydro jetting works and when we avoid it

A hose with a specialized nozzle goes into the line through a cleanout. Water pushed at high pressure sprays backward and forward, scouring the wall and flushing debris toward the main. We stop and reassess if the pipe looks fragile.

  • Camera first, to confirm the pipe is intact.
  • Jetting from a cleanout, not from a toilet or fixture.
  • Camera again, to show the result.
  • Written note on remaining defects, so you know what is next.

Jetting alongside other drain services

Jetting is not a replacement for snaking. Often the sequence is cable first, camera second, jetting third. A kitchen branch might need only drain cleaning, while a main line with repeat problems suits sewer line cleaning with jetting.

After jetting, a camera pass confirms the pipe wall is clean and shows any defects that were hiding behind the buildup. Then the choice is yours: monitor, schedule maintenance, or repair.

When jetting is the wrong tool

Hydro jetting is powerful, and power is not always what a pipe needs. A cracked or collapsed pipe can be damaged further by high pressure. A very old line with thin walls may not tolerate it. That is why we look with a camera first, and why we may recommend a cable machine on a fragile line. A good plumber chooses the tool to suit the pipe, not the other way around.

Keeping a jetted line clean

  • Scrape grease and food into the trash, never down the drain.
  • Keep wipes, paper towels and feminine products out of the toilet.
  • Schedule maintenance jetting for restaurants and buildings with a history of grease buildup.
  • Ask for the camera footage after the job so you can see the clean pipe.

Roots regrow. If jetting clears roots from an otherwise sound line, expect to repeat it at an interval your plumber recommends. A line that needs clearing every few months may need sewer line repair instead.

Can jetting damage my pipes?

On a sound pipe, no. We camera first and skip jetting on fragile or broken lines.

How long does the result last?

It depends on the cause. A clean pipe wall stays clear much longer than a cabled hole.

Is jetting safe for septic systems?

It depends on the system. We discuss it before work.

Can you jet a kitchen line?

Yes, through a cleanout where available.
