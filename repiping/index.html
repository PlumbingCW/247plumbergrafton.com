Signs your Grafton home needs repiping

One leak is a repair. Repeated leaks in different places are a pattern. Pipes age from the inside, and when one section fails, the rest are usually close behind. Repiping is a bigger job than patching, but it ends the cycle of repairs, stained ceilings and surprise water bills.

  • Leaks in more than one location within a few years.
  • Brown or rusty water when you first open a tap.
  • Falling pressure at fixtures that used to run strong.
  • Visible green corrosion or white mineral crust on pipes.
  • Galvanized steel or old polybutylene in the house.
  • Pinhole leaks in copper.

If you only have a single failed section, burst pipe repair is the right first step, and we will tell you honestly whether the rest of the system looks sound.

Copper or PEX

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
Copper.Long track record, fire resistant, no plastic taste.Higher material cost, more labor, can pinhole in aggressive water.
PEX.Flexible, fewer fittings, fast to run through walls.Must be protected from sunlight and some chemicals.
CPVC.Low cost for some installs.Can become brittle with age.

We recommend by house, not by habit. A tight plaster-and-lath wall is a good case for PEX because it bends around obstacles. An exposed basement run might suit copper. We show you the options and the reason for each.

How repiping works with minimal mess

  • Walk-through and a pipe map of the whole house.
  • Water shut off in sections, with a plan to restore water each evening where possible.
  • Small access holes rather than opening whole walls where we can.
  • New lines run, pressure tested and connected to fixtures.
  • Patch holes ready for paint, or coordinate with your drywall contractor.

All work is by licensed plumbers under the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, and permit rules apply. Ask the Grafton building department about permitting for your address, since the town runs online permitting. You can verify any plumber through the state license lookup.

Repiping older Grafton houses

Grafton has housing from several eras. Homes near the historic districts of Fisherville and Farnumsville, including former mill housing, can have galvanized or early copper lines. Houses built later have newer materials but may still have old fittings. Because the town draws on groundwater from Grafton Water District, South Grafton Water District or private wells, water chemistry differs by address.

We do not quote a hardness number. A water test or your district annual report tells you what your pipes live with.

If your repiping plan includes a bathroom or kitchen update, ask about plumbing installation so the work happens once.

Living in the house during a repipe

Most homeowners stay in the house through a repipe. We plan the work in sections so water is restored in the evening where possible. Expect noise, dust at the access points and furniture moved away from the work areas. Pets should be kept clear. Tell us about anyone who depends on running water at certain hours, and we will plan around them.

Planning around the seasons

Repiping can happen in any season, but cold weather adds risk if water must stay off for long. Many homeowners schedule it for spring or fall.
