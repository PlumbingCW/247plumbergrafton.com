Signs the service line is leaking

A water service line leak is usually underground. Look for low pressure that came on gradually, a wet or sunken spot between the street and the house, a meter that moves when everything is off, or a sudden jump in the water bill. In winter, a service line can freeze if the burial depth is shallow.

  • Meter moves with every fixture off.
  • Soft spot, puddle or green stripe in the lawn.
  • Reduced pressure at every fixture.
  • Water noise near the entry point of the house.

If you suspect a service line leak, close the main valve inside the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is outside it. See leak detection for how we narrow the location.

Grafton has two public water systems. If you are in South Grafton, read how its water district works before you plan work on the line to your meter.

Repair, replacement and who owns what

Ownership matters. In most places the water utility owns the pipe up to the meter, and you own the line from the meter to the house. Rules differ between Grafton Water District, South Grafton Water District, and private wells, so call your water utility to confirm where the dividing line is on your property.

If the leak is on your side, we locate it and then decide on a repair. A short section can be spot repaired. Old galvanized or lead-era lines are better replaced entirely. Replacement uses modern pipe, with a trench or a trenchless method where suitable.

Planning the work in Grafton

Digging up a service line affects lawns, driveways and sometimes landscaping. We explain the path before we start and mark what will be affected. For work in the public way or involving the meter, the water utility has to be involved, and we coordinate the pieces that are ours. Permit rules vary, so ask the Grafton building department about your address.

Homes in North Grafton and South Grafton may sit on different water districts, and some homes use private wells. A well line has no meter, so we rely on pressure testing and listening gear. Frozen service lines are covered on our burst pipe repair page.

Before the excavator arrives

Call 811 so public utilities are marked, and ask your water district who owns each part of the line. Locate the shutoff at the curb and at the house. If a trench crosses a driveway or walkway, ask the plumber about restoration, because paving costs often surprise people. A good quote lists excavation, pipe, fittings, backfill and surface repair as separate items.

Temporary water during the repair

Ask whether the plumber can restore water to the house the same day. Many repairs finish quickly. If not, plan for bottled water and a way to flush toilets.

Questions about water service line repair in Grafton

Do you handle water service line emergencies at night or on weekends in Grafton?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line. We advise you to close the main valve.

Who is responsible for the water service line?

It depends on where the meter or property line sits. Call your water utility to confirm.

Can a service line be replaced without digging up the yard?

Sometimes, with trenchless methods. A site visit tells us.

Why is my water pressure low?

Causes include a partial leak, a failing pressure regulator, or a clogged line. We test and tell you which.

Does a private well use a service line?

Yes. The line from the well or pressure tank to the house is similar, with different tests.
