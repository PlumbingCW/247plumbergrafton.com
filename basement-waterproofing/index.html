Where does your basement water come from?

Water enters a basement from a few sources, and the repair depends on which one. Treating the wrong cause wastes money. We check each before recommending work.

SourceCluesTypical response
Surface water.Wet after storms, near downspouts.Gutters, downspout extensions, grading.

Sump systems and interior drainage

An interior drain collects water at the footing and sends it to a sump pit, where a pump discharges it outside. It works with the water table rather than fighting it. The system is only as good as the pump, the discharge line and the power, so we also look at battery backup. See sump pump repair if your pump is already installed.

  • Pit sized for the inflow.
  • Check valve on the discharge.
  • Discharge routed away from the foundation.
  • Battery backup for outages.
  • Alarm for high water.

We are plumbers, not structural engineers. If you have large foundation cracks, bowing walls or settlement, we say so and recommend a structural specialist.

What you can do first

  • Clean gutters and extend downspouts several feet from the house.
  • Check that the ground slopes away from the foundation.
  • Move stored items off the floor.
  • Test the sump pump by pouring water into the pit.
  • Photograph damage and keep dry-out receipts for insurance.

Standard homeowners policies often exclude groundwater seepage and may cover sudden pipe failures. Ask your carrier which applies to your loss.

Basements in Grafton

Grafton sits in the Blackstone River watershed, and basements near Fisherville, Farnumsville and low parts of town can see high groundwater after spring thaw and heavy storms. The town has wetlands and brooks that influence local drainage. Houses with older stone or block foundations behave differently from newer poured walls. Because conditions vary by lot, we inspect rather than guess.

Homes with finished basements deserve extra caution. A plumbing leak behind drywall can look like groundwater. A leak check first can save a large project.

Finished basements and what hides behind the drywall

A finished basement hides problems. Water that seeps behind paneling or drywall feeds mold long before a stain shows. If you smell mustiness, see paint bubbling near the floor, or find damp carpet edges, open a small inspection hole before you spend money on a system. Ask a plumber to rule out supply and drain leaks first, since a pinhole in a pipe inside a wall looks exactly like groundwater.

Moisture meters and a simple plastic sheet test help. Tape a square of clear plastic to the wall for two days. Moisture under the plastic comes through the wall. Moisture on the room side of the plastic is humidity in the air.

Questions to ask any waterproofing contractor

  • Which source of water did you find, and how do you know?
  • Does the quote include the pump, battery backup and discharge line?
  • Where does the water go once it leaves the pit?
  • What is the warranty, and does it transfer if I sell?
  • What happens to the finished wall if you need to cut it?

A company that quotes a full interior system without finding the source is selling a product, not a diagnosis. We start with the source and tell you if the fix is as simple as extending a downspout.
